How spaced repetition works

Spaced repetition is an evidence-based learning technique, usually done with flashcards. New and difficult words come up often, while words you already know come up less and less. This uses the spacing effect: we remember things better when reviews are spread out over time than when they are crammed together.

It works best when you need to learn many items and keep them for good, which makes it a natural fit for building vocabulary in a new language. Every review also tests you, and recalling a word yourself makes it stick far better than just reading it again. PawlaPalabra takes care of the schedule, so you only need to show up when your words are due.

The schedule in PawlaPalabra

Every word climbs the same ladder. After each session without mistakes, it moves up one stage and the wait until its next review gets longer:

StageSessionNext review in
0You learn the word1 day
11st review3 days
22nd review7 days
33rd review14 days
44th review30 days
55th reviewLearned

After the 5th review the word is learned, and it no longer comes back for repetition.

Made a mistake? If you get a word wrong during a session, it goes back to the beginning of the ladder and comes back tomorrow. It then climbs again from stage 0.

That is why the app sometimes tells you there is nothing to repeat yet: your words are simply waiting for their next review day.
